Choosing the right visual Synthetic example data

Every figure below is a form picked for its task, with the reason stated. All numbers are invented for demonstration and labeled as such. Guidance: SKILL.md, Step 9 - "Charts, graphs, cards and diagrams".

1. Comparing values: bar chart

Task: "which plan is most popular?" Bars make magnitude readable at a glance; values are labeled directly so the axis is a backup, not the only source.

2. Change over time: line chart

Task: "are we getting faster?" A line shows the trend and the turning point; bars would invite reading each month in isolation.

3. Exact values: a table wins

Task: "what exactly did each region sell?" When the job is looking up a number, rows and aligned figures beat every chart.

4. Process: a diagram, not a chart

Task: "how does an order move?" Steps and handoffs want boxes and arrows in reading order. The flow is also described in text for assistive tech.

Cart→ Payment→ Packed→ Shipped→ Delivered

5. One takeaway: plain text

Task: "what's the headline?" A single important number is a sentence, not a visualization.

Support first-response time is down 38% since June (synthetic).

6. Cards: only for independent peers

Three self-contained, repeatable items - that is what cards are for. One chart or one paragraph never goes in a card.
